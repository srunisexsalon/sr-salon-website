Wanting a colour change is one thing — knowing what'll actually suit you is another. Before we pick up any colour, we look at what you're working with: your current shade, hair condition, how much of a change you're really after.

Sometimes it's a small refresh. Sometimes it's a complete shift. Either way, the starting condition of your hair matters more than people expect — previous colouring, bleaching, smoothing treatments, all of it affects what's realistic.

Global Colour vs Highlights

Global colour means one even shade throughout — good for a full refresh or covering grey. Highlights add contrast in specific sections instead of changing everything. Neither is "better," it depends what look you're going for.

Lighter starting hair usually shows colour changes more dramatically than darker hair — worth knowing before you set expectations too high for one session.

Covering Grey Hair

Probably the most common reason people come in for colour. How much maintenance it needs really depends on how much grey you're covering and how fast your roots grow back in. We'll talk through what a realistic touch-up schedule looks like for you specifically.

Picking a Shade That Actually Works

Some people want something close to their natural colour. Others want something bolder. Either way, your hair's current condition and your skin tone both play into what'll actually look good — not just what's trending.

If your hair's already been through a lot of chemical treatments, mention that upfront. It changes how we approach the colouring process.

Keeping Colour Looking Good After

Coloured hair needs slightly different care — right shampoo, less aggressive heat styling, that kind of thing. We'll give you pointers based on what treatment you got, not a generic care sheet.

If you're doing regular touch-ups for roots or grey coverage, we can talk about a maintenance schedule that actually fits your hair's growth rate.

Why Come to Us for This

We'd rather ask a few questions and get the shade right than rush into something that doesn't work with your hair. Men and women both come in for colour here — whatever the reason, tell us what you're picturing.
