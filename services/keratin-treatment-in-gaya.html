What is keratin treatment?

Keratin treatment is a salon hair treatment designed to improve the smoothness and manageability of the hair. It can help create a smoother-looking finish and reduce the appearance of frizz.

Is keratin the same as hair straightening?

No. Keratin and hair straightening are different services. Keratin is generally chosen for a smoother and more manageable appearance, while straightening is focused more directly on creating a straighter hair finish.

Can keratin treatment be done on coloured hair?

It may be possible depending on the condition of the hair and the previous colour or chemical treatment. Always tell the stylist about your hair history before the service so the treatment can be assessed properly.

How long does a keratin treatment appointment take?

Appointment time depends on hair length, volume, texture, condition and the treatment process. Longer or thicker hair may require more time. The stylist can give you a better estimate during consultation.

Is keratin suitable for every hair type?

The suitability of a keratin treatment depends on the individual hair condition, previous treatments and the result you want. A consultation is useful before deciding on the service.

What Does Keratin Treatment Do?

A keratin treatment is designed to improve the smoothness and manageability of the hair. Depending on the treatment and the individual's hair, it can help reduce the appearance of frizz and create a smoother-looking finish. The result is not identical for everyone because natural hair texture, condition and the treatment technique all influence the final appearance.

Many clients choose keratin because they want their daily hair routine to be easier. Hair that is easier to comb and style can save time during everyday preparation. The service can also give the hair a more polished appearance when you are getting ready for work, college, parties or special occasions.

Keratin Treatment for Frizzy Hair

Frizz can make the hair look uneven and difficult to control, particularly in humid weather. A smoothing treatment such as keratin may be considered by clients who want a more controlled and polished appearance.

However, the amount of smoothing depends on the hair's natural texture and the treatment used. It is better to discuss your expected result with the stylist instead of assuming that every keratin treatment will produce exactly the same finish.

Keratin Treatment for Curly and Wavy Hair

Curly and wavy hair can have different styling requirements depending on its texture and thickness. Some clients want to keep some of their natural movement while making the hair easier to manage, while others prefer a smoother overall appearance.

During consultation, you can explain how much change you want. The stylist can then discuss whether keratin or another hair service is more appropriate for your desired finish.

Keratin Treatment vs Hair Straightening

Keratin treatment and hair straightening should not be considered the same service. Keratin is generally selected by people looking for smoother, softer and more manageable hair, while straightening treatments are designed with a stronger focus on creating a straighter appearance.

Choosing between the two depends on your natural hair texture and the result you want. If you are unsure, a consultation with a professional stylist can help you understand the differences before choosing a service.

Keratin Treatment vs Hair Spa

Hair spa and keratin treatment have different purposes. Hair spa is generally selected for conditioning and regular hair care, while keratin treatment is chosen when the client wants a smoother and more manageable hair appearance.

If your main concern is dryness or the need for routine conditioning, a hair spa may be worth considering. If your main concern is frizz and manageability, you can discuss a keratin treatment with the stylist.

Keratin for Coloured or Chemically Treated Hair

Clients who have coloured, highlighted, bleached or chemically treated hair should mention their previous treatments before booking a keratin service. The current condition of the hair is an important factor when deciding whether a treatment is appropriate.

Hair that has already undergone multiple chemical services may require additional consideration. Instead of applying the same treatment to every client, the stylist should first understand the hair's history and condition.

Keratin Treatment According to Hair Length

Hair length and volume can affect both the treatment time and the amount of product required. Short hair may need less working time, while long or thick hair can take longer to prepare and treat.

This is one reason why keratin pricing can vary between clients. The final cost may depend on factors such as hair length, density, condition and the treatment requirements discussed during the consultation.

What to Discuss Before Keratin Treatment

Before starting the service, tell your stylist about any recent hair colour, bleaching, straightening, smoothing or other chemical treatment. It is also useful to explain whether you want a very smooth finish or prefer to retain some natural movement in the hair.

You can also show a reference photograph if you have a particular finish in mind. A reference helps the stylist understand your expectations, although the final result may naturally differ depending on your own hair.
